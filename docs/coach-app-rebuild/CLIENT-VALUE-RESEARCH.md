# What clients value: outside research (21 November 2026)

This feeds the coach-app build plan. The feature study (`FEATURE-STUDY.md`) looked inward at
294 existing features. This looks outward: published studies, platform docs, real reviews and
Indian sources on remote 3–6 month nutrition and coaching programmes.

**How it was done.** Four research agents, one each on studies, platforms and reviews,
referrals, and India. Searches used generic terms only. Most studies were read from the abstract;
a few were read in full. Reddit, G2 and Capterra blocked automated reading, so review evidence
comes from the App Store, Trustpilot and complaint sites. Samples there are small.

**The short version.** The outside evidence says what the seven persona clients said: a plan
built for *them*, the same person every time, a real human who answers, and no sales pressure.
It adds four things the study missed:

- the client is often not the cook;
- fasting and festival days are where plans fail;
- clients want to *see* their before and after;
- a clear written record of what was agreed after each call.

## 1. Top 10 things clients value, ranked

| # | What clients value | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A plan built around them, not a generic sheet**: their food, their routine, practical meals. | 2 | **A real person who is paying attention and replies.** The top complaint across every platform is a coach who goes quiet or sounds scripted. | [Noom App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/noom-weight-loss-health/id634598719), [VICE](https://vice.com/en/article/does-the-noom-app-actually-help-you-lose-weight), [HealthifyMe reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/943712366/healthifyme-weight-loss-plan/reviews), [Nutrium App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/nutrium/id1136652584) |
| 3 | **The same practitioner, who knows their history.** Continuity is tied to satisfaction; swapped coaches are a top complaint. | [JHN 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jhn.70056), [systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1490082), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.healthifyme.com?page=2) |
| 4 | **Being listened to and given clear explanations.** These are the strongest drivers of "would recommend": listening has an odds ratio of 5.41, explaining clearly 2.49. | [PMC10831569](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10831569), [MGMA](https://www.mgma.com/articles/onboarding-providers-to-prioritize-patient-experience) |

| 6 | **Accountability with reassurance**: regular contact, problem-solving, feedback. | 7 | **Advice that reaches whoever actually cooks, in their language.** An Indian trial sent diet messages to the family cook in English, Hindi and Punjabi; intake improved about 20%. | 8 | **Seeing their own progress over time, as information, not pressure.** Slow early results predict drop-out. | [Noom App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/noom-weight-loss-health/id634598719), trial n=771 ([Obes Facts](https://doi.org/10.1159/000345951)) |

| 10 | **Trust: no pressure-selling, no money surprises, supplements justified.** 59% of Indians fear supplement side effects. "Limited-time offer" pushes and surprise charges are named in reviews. | [Mintel](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/vms-consumption-helps-75-indians-achieve-overall-health-and-wellness-52-driven-by-organic-natural-claim), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.healthifyme.com?page=2), [PMC9759390](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9759390) |

Remote delivery itself is not a problem. 92.7% were satisfied with video and phone
consultations with allied health professionals ([n=124](https://doi.org/10.5114/wo.2022.115047)).

**What drives drop-out.** These two are worth knowing because they come before any referral:

- Expectations set too high at the start ([n=1,785](https://doi.org/10.1038/oby.2005.241), [n=634](https://doi.org/10.1159/000441366)).
- Slow early results ([n=771](https://doi.org/10.1159/000345951)).
- Low mood or living alone ([n=250](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40519-020-00990-9)).
- A family not on board ([review of 23 studies](https://doi.org/10.1089/chi.2014.0060)).
- Disliked food, the cost of food and supplements, and not understanding the diet ([n=27](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40795-021-00480-w)).

## 2. What drives referrals, and what kills them

**What makes clients recommend.** Strongest evidence first:

1. **They felt listened to, and things were explained clearly.** This is the single strongest
   link to recommending ([PMC10831569](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10831569)).
2. **A friend mentions a need.** Advisers' clients referred mostly when a friend described a
   problem (57%) or asked for a name (48%). Only 2% referred because they were asked. 91% were
   willing but only 29% had referred; some feared it would reflect badly on them
   ([survey](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/client-relations/clients-aren-t-pulling-trigger-on-referrals-survey-says)).
   This is financial advice, not health, but the pattern is clear.
3. **The same person throughout.** In one clinic, people who would warn friends off had seen
   their own provider only 3.7% of the time ([JABFM](https://www.jabfm.org/content/17/5/341.full)).
4. **Perceived value and the quality of each contact** ([PMC5800231](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5800231/)).
5. **Visible results.** These raise recommendation in app-based care, where the experience is
   thinner than in-person ([JMIR Rehab](https://rehab.jmir.org/2022/1/e31349/)). Clients who
   succeeded wanted to send family and friends ([JHN 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jhn.70056)).
6. **The ending.** It matters, but less than people assume: a hotel field study found first
   impressions mattered most ([J Bus Res](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v185y2024ics014829632400403x.html),
   [colonoscopy peak-end](https://sparq.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj19021/files/media/file/redelmeier_et_al._2003_-_memories_of_colonoscopy.pdf)).
   So **Day 1 and week 1 deserve as much care as graduation.**

**What kills a recommendation:**

- **Manner, not clinical skill.** 59–78% of complaints in negative reviews are non-clinical ([PMC3358396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3358396)).
- **Feeling rushed** ([PMC9759390](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9759390)).
- **Slow access or waiting** ([PMC9213250](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9213250)).
- **Money surprises and sales pressure** ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.healthifyme.com?page=2)).
- **A changed practitioner** ([JABFM](https://www.jabfm.org/content/17/5/341.full)).
- **A failure followed by a recovery.** Fixing it raises satisfaction but not word of mouth ([meta-analysis](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1094670510387914)), so preventing the failure matters more than apologising well.

**Asking, incentives and testimonials:**

- No study found that asking for referrals helps in health care. The adviser survey suggests it barely works.
- Rewards make clients worry how they look to the friend, most of all with close friends ([NUS](https://web2-bschool.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/media_rp/publications/x1WUW1528209854.pdf)).
- In India, ASCI treats testimonials as not proof of a health claim ([ASCI](https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code-guidelines/)).
- NBHWC requires coaches to disclose up front any income from products they sell to clients ([NBHWC](https://nbhwc.org/code-of-ethics/)). This is relevant to supplement buy links that pay commission.

This fits the no-chasing rule: **referrals come from the work itself, not from asking.** The
one thing a practice can add is making it easy for a happy client to say, in a line, who the
coach helps and with what. They can then pass that on when a friend mentions a need.

## 4. Features worth adding to the coach-app build plan

1. **Reply clock on Today (coach-side).** Each unanswered client message shows how long it has
   waited, oldest first, and turns amber near the 24-hour promise the client app already makes.
   This is the single biggest complaint across every platform, and the cheapest fix. Nothing
   is sent to anyone.
2. **Before-and-after view, released by the coach.** Fill the "Your progress" sheet:
   - week 1 against now, from the client's own dated records (symptom scores, weight, blood
     pressure, released labs);
   - improvements first, then what is still moving;
   - plain numbers with no evidence hedging;
   - the coach releases it, the same way she releases results.

   The coach side shows the same view on the Brief, so she sees it before a review call. It
   also covers study gap 7 (outcomes) and gives the client something real to show a friend.
3. **"What we agreed" call note.** After each call the coach writes three or four lines in
   chat or on the Brief: the decisions, the changes, and what she promised. It appears as a
   dated card in the client's Coach tab. Her promises also land on her own to-do list (study
   gap 7). This covers clear explanation, shared decisions and the "dated records" the persona
   clients asked for. It is coach-written and nothing automatic.
4. **Cook's sheet in the new app, for the whole household.**
   - Link the existing bilingual sheet from the new menu and grocery screens.
   - Set the cook's language per client: Hindi now; Marathi, Tamil and the rest later,
     authored in chat at $0.
   - Add an optional one-line "family version" per dish, so one meal is cooked and the
     client's portion is adjusted.

   This answers the SMART Eating trial and the joint-family evidence.
5. **Fasting and festival days per client.**
   - Intake asks which fasts the client keeps: Navratri, Ekadashi, Karva Chauth, Ramadan,
     Paryushan, weekly vrat.
   - The menu drafter plans fast-safe meals for those dates.
   - The supplement timing for fasting days is checked.
   - The coach sees "fast coming up" on the client's Brief.

   It shows in the client's own menu, not as a message, so it is no nudge. It covers the
   days plans fail and clients do not raise.

The research also supports two items already on the study's gaps list, and they should stay
high in the build plan: the reaction log with a "stop this supplement" step, and programme
payment status (no money surprises).
