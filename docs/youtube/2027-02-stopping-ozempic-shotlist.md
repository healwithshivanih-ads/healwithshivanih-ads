# Stopping Ozempic? Watch This Before You Do: voiceover and shot list

The script itself (`2027-02-stopping-ozempic-script.md`) is approved. **The words below are the same words**, broken into shots. Don't reword the numbers; they've been checked against the papers.

## How to read this

Each shot is marked with one of these:
- **ON CAM**: you, speaking to camera. Film these in one session.
- **VO**: your voice only, recorded separately, played over footage or graphics.

Inside the words:
- **Bold** = lean on this word a little.
- [pause] = a one-beat pause (about half a second). Let it breathe.

What goes on screen:
- **Stock** = search terms for a stock-footage site (Pexels and Pixabay are free; Storyblocks is paid).
- **AI clip** = a prompt for an AI video tool (Runway, Higgsfield or similar). Ask for 5–8 second clips in 16:9.
- **Graphic** = an animated card or chart you make in Canva (or have an editor make).
- **Text** = words that appear on screen. These are the "stickers" from the script.

**Running time:** about 14 minutes. Roughly 45% on camera and 55% voiceover.

---

## Visual rules for the whole video

1. **No brand names or logos on screen.** Show generic, unbranded injection pens only. Say "Ozempic" out loud, but never show the packaging.
2. **No body-shaming footage.** No headless bellies, no pinching fat, no "before/after" bodies. Show people *doing* things: cooking, lifting, walking, sleeping.
3. **Show real, varied people,** mostly women aged 35–60, from a range of backgrounds, including Indian faces.
4. **One colour palette for graphics.** Use your brand ochre and warm neutrals. Show "regain" numbers in a muted terracotta and "kept off" numbers in a calm green.
5. **Every statistic card shows its source** in small text at the bottom (author, journal, year). Viewers trust it more, and it protects you.

## 1 · Why the weight comes back (1:00 – 2:30)

**Shot 7 · 1:00–1:05 · ON CAM**
> To understand regain, you need to understand what these drugs actually do.

**Shot 8 · 1:05–1:22 · VO**
> GLP-1 is a hormone your **own** gut makes after you eat. It tells your brain, "we've had enough," and slows down how fast your stomach empties. Drugs like semaglutide and tirzepatide copy that signal, but much **stronger** and for much **longer**.

- Graphic: a simple animated diagram (no anatomy gore). Gut → a glowing signal travels up → brain icon shows a "full" tick. Then a second, much brighter, longer-lasting signal labelled "medicine".

**Shot 9 · 1:22–1:38 · VO**
> So the simplest way to think about it: the medicine turns the volume on hunger right **down**. A lot of people describe it as the "food noise" going quiet for the first time in years.
>
> When you stop, the volume comes back **up**. [pause] Often to where it was before.

- AI clip: *"Close-up of a hand turning a vintage brass volume knob slowly down to zero, warm light, shallow depth of field, cinematic, 16:9"*. Then the same knob turning back **up** for "the volume comes back up".

**Shot 10 · 1:38–1:58 · VO**

## 2 · The honest numbers (2:30 – 5:00)

**Shot 12 · 2:30–2:33 · ON CAM**
> Let's start with the clinical trials.

**Shot 13 · 2:33–2:52 · VO**
> In the semaglutide trial called STEP-1, people lost about 17% of their body weight over 68 weeks. Then they stopped the drug and were followed for a year. In that year, they regained about **two-thirds** of what they'd lost.

- Graphic: a line chart. Weight drops from 0% to −17% across "68 weeks on the drug", then climbs back to about −6% over "1 year off". Shade the regained part in terracotta.
- Text: **Semaglutide: ~⅔ of lost weight regained within 1 year of stopping.**
- Source line: *Wilding et al., Diabetes Obes Metab 2022 (STEP-1 extension)*
- Accuracy note: lost 17.3%, regained 11.6 points, finished 5.6% below the start. Plot these figures.

**Shot 14 · 2:52–3:12 · VO**
> With tirzepatide, the drug in Mounjaro and Zepbound, a trial called SURMOUNT-4 lost about 21% first. Then half the group was quietly switched to a placebo. Over the next year, that group regained about 14% of their body weight, roughly **half** of what they'd lost.

- Graphic: the same chart style. Drop to −21%, then the line **splits in two**. "Stayed on the drug" keeps going down (to about −25%). "Switched to placebo" climbs back to about −10%.
- Text: **Tirzepatide: ~half regained within 1 year of switching to placebo.**
- Source line: *Aronne et al., JAMA 2024 (SURMOUNT-4)*
- Accuracy note: lost 20.9% first; the placebo group regained 14.0%; the drug group lost a further 5.5%.

**Shot 15 · 3:12–3:30 · VO**
> Then, in January 2026, Oxford researchers pulled together 37 studies with more than 9,000 people. For the newer drugs, weight came back at about 0.8 kg a month, almost 2 pounds, and was projected to be back at the starting point in about a year and a half.

- Source line: *West et al., BMJ 2026. Newer-drug figures come from 8 studies with up to 12 months' follow-up; later figures are projections.*

## 3 · The hidden problem: what you actually lost (5:00 – 6:45)

**Shot 23 · 5:00–5:05 · ON CAM**
> Before the plan, there's one thing almost nobody talks about.

**Shot 24 · 5:05–5:35 · VO**
> When you lose weight quickly, not all of it is fat. In the STEP-1 trial, a group of participants had body scans. About 38% of the weight they lost was what the scan calls "lean mass".
>
> Now, lean mass isn't only muscle. It also includes water, organs and other tissue. When experts estimate actual muscle, it comes to roughly one-fifth of the weight lost: around 10 to 15% for women and 20 to 25% for men, when there's no structured strength training. Still, that's a meaningful amount of tissue you want to keep.

- AI clip: *"Medical DXA body-composition scanner arm moving slowly over a person lying on a padded table, clinical but calm, soft blue and cream light, no visible face, 16:9"*
- Stock alternative: "DEXA scan", "body composition scan".
- Graphic: a pie of "weight lost". 62% fat (ochre), 38% lean mass (grey). Then the lean slice splits: about a fifth of the total pie glows as "muscle".
- Text: **STEP-1: ~38% of weight lost was "lean mass"; roughly ⅕ was actual muscle (10–15% in women, 20–25% in men, without strength training).**
- Source line: *Mozaffarian et al., Obesity Pillars 2025 (summarising STEP-1, Wilding et al., NEJM 2021)*

**Shot 25 · 5:35–5:50 · VO**
> Here's why that matters for regain. Muscle is your metabolic **engine**. It burns energy at rest, it helps your body handle blood sugar, and it keeps you strong as you age.

- Stock: "woman 50s lifting dumbbells at home", "older woman carrying groceries up stairs", "grandmother lifting child".

**Shot 26 · 5:50–6:30 · ON CAM**
> If you lose muscle on the way down and then regain weight, the evidence on weight cycling suggests the weight that comes back **tends** to be more fat than muscle. So you can end up at the same number on the scale, but with a body that's weaker and less metabolically healthy than when you started.

- Edit: on "same number on the scale", a 2-second cutaway: stock "bathroom scale feet close up" (feet only, no body). Then back to you.
- ⚠ Keep the word **"tends"**. That claim is still waiting for a primary citation, so don't strengthen it on camera.

**Shot 27 · 6:30–6:45 · ON CAM, brighter**
> The good news: this is one of the most **preventable** problems in the whole picture. Which brings us to the plan.

- Edit: the music lifts into the plan section.

## 4 · Your soft-landing plan (6:45 – 11:45)

**Shot 28 · 6:45–7:05 · ON CAM**
> I call this a soft landing. Most of these steps work best if you start **while you're still on the medicine**, not after. The drug quiets your appetite, which makes it the easiest time to build new habits. Think of it as training wheels you use to learn the bike, so you can still ride when they come off.

- Edit: a 2-second cutaway on "training wheels". AI clip: *"Child's bicycle with training wheels on a sunny suburban path, a parent's hand letting go of the seat, warm golden light, cinematic, 16:9"* or stock "learning to ride a bike training wheels".
- Text: **Your soft-landing plan** (section title card).

**Shot 29 · 7:05–7:20 · VO**
> That isn't just my opinion. In 2025, four major obesity and nutrition organisations published joint guidance for people on these medicines, and this was one of their key points.

- Graphic: a quote card, typed out word by word: **"Establish positive nutrition and other lifestyle practices before and during GLP-1 therapy, to increase success at maintaining such practices if the drug is stopped."**
- Source line: *Mozaffarian et al., joint advisory (ACLM, ASN, OMA, TOS), Obesity Pillars 2025*
- Edit: hold the card at least 6 seconds so people can read it.

### Step 1 · Protein

**Shot 30 · 7:20–7:28 · ON CAM**
> Step one: protein, at every meal. When your appetite is low, protein is often the first thing to drop. Yet protein is what tells your body to hold on to muscle.

- Text: **1 · Protein at every meal**
- Note: "Step one:" (and "Step two:" to "Step five:", plus "One:" to "Four:" in Shot 44) are the only words added to the approved script. They're spoken signposts for the viewer. Skip them if you'd rather not.

**Shot 31 · 7:28–7:55 · VO**
> That same 2025 guidance notes that, during weight loss, targets of about 1.2 to 1.6 grams of protein per kilo of body weight per day have been proposed. It also offers a simpler number: [pause] roughly **80 to 120 grams a day**. If you're carrying a lot of extra weight, ask your doctor or dietitian which number fits you, because calculating from your full body weight can overestimate what you need.

- Stock: "weighing food on kitchen scale", "woman reading food label".
- Text: **Protein during weight loss: ~1.2–1.6 g per kg per day, or simply ~80–120 g a day, spread across meals.**
- Source line: *Mozaffarian et al., Obesity Pillars 2025*

**Shot 32 · 7:55–8:15 · VO**
> Don't try to get it all in one meal. Spread it across the day: about 25 to 30 grams at each main meal.
>
> What does that look like? Two or three eggs. A bowl of Greek yoghurt. A palm-and-a-half of fish or chicken. A cup of cooked lentils with some tofu or paneer. A protein-rich breakfast is the one most people miss.

- Stock, cut on each food as you name it: "eggs frying pan overhead", "greek yogurt bowl berries", "grilled fish plate", "dal lentils bowl", "paneer tikka" / "tofu stir fry".
- AI clip alternative: *"Overhead shot of three plated meals on a wooden table: scrambled eggs with toast, a bowl of thick yoghurt with seeds, and a bowl of dal with paneer, natural daylight, food photography, 16:9"*
- Graphic: a small "≈ 25–30 g protein" tag pops onto each plate.

### Step 2 · Strength training

**Shot 33 · 8:15–8:35 · ON CAM**
> Step two: strength training, at least three times a week. Protein is the building material. Strength training is the **signal** to use it.
>
> The same guidance recommends strength training **at least three times a week**, plus about 150 minutes of moderate activity like brisk walking, to protect muscle and bone. And it says plainly that extra protein on its own is unlikely to be enough without the strength training.

- Text: **2 · Strength training ≥3×/week**
- Text (later in the shot): **Strength training ≥3×/week + ~150 min moderate activity. Protein alone isn't enough.** Source line: *Mozaffarian et al., Obesity Pillars 2025*

**Shot 34 · 8:35–8:58 · VO**
> You don't need a gym membership or heavy barbells to start. Squats to a chair, wall push-ups, resistance bands, carrying shopping bags. What matters is that your muscles work against resistance regularly, and that it gets a little harder over time. If three times feels like a lot right now, start with two and build up.

- Stock, cut on each exercise as you name it: "woman chair squat at home", "wall push up", "resistance band exercise living room", "woman carrying grocery bags".
- Alternative: **film these four yourself** on your phone, which is more personal. Each one takes about 5 seconds.

### Step 3 · Fibre

**Shot 35 · 8:58–9:10 · ON CAM**
> Step three: fibre. Remember, GLP-1 is a hormone your **own** gut makes. One of the ways it does this is through fibre.

- Text: **3 · Fibre: help your body make its own GLP-1**

**Shot 36 · 9:10–9:30 · VO**
> When you eat fibre from beans, lentils, oats, vegetables, fruit, nuts and seeds, the bacteria in your gut ferment it into compounds called short-chain fatty acids. In human studies, one of these, propionate, increased the body's own release of GLP-1 and another fullness hormone called PYY, and people ate less.

- Stock: "beans lentils oats overhead", "colourful vegetables chopping board", "nuts and seeds bowls".
- AI clip: *"Stylised animated gut microbes as soft glowing spheres breaking down fibre strands and releasing tiny golden particles, friendly scientific illustration style, warm palette, 16:9"*
- Text: **Gut bacteria turn fibre into short-chain fatty acids, which raise your body's own GLP-1 and PYY.**
- Source line: *Chambers et al., Gut 2015*

**Shot 37 · 9:30–9:55 · ON CAM, honest tone**
> Let me be really clear: your natural GLP-1 is much weaker than the drug. Fibre will **not** replace the medicine. But it works *with* your own appetite system rather than against it. That's exactly what you want when the drug is no longer doing the work.
>
> If you've seen "fibremaxxing" on social media, this is the sensible version of it: build up slowly, aim for fibre at every meal, and drink enough water.

- Text (small, on "fibremaxxing"): **#fibremaxxing, the sensible version**

### Step 4 · Sleep and stress

**Shot 38 · 9:55–10:00 · ON CAM**
> Step four: sleep and stress. This one surprises people.

- Text: **4 · Sleep and stress**

**Shot 39 · 10:00–10:18 · VO**
> In a well-known study, healthy young men slept only four hours a night for two nights. Their hunger hormone, ghrelin, went up by about 28%, their fullness hormone, leptin, went down, and their appetite for sweet and starchy foods jumped by a third or more.

- Stock: "man awake at night alarm clock 3am", "tired person looking at phone in bed".
- Graphic: two hormone bars. Ghrelin **↑28%** (terracotta), leptin ↓ (grey). Then a "sweet and starchy cravings ↑33–45%" icon.

- Source line: *Spiegel et al., Ann Intern Med 2004 (small study; later research mixed, but most points the same way)*

**Shot 40 · 10:18–10:35 · VO**
> Chronic stress does something similar. So if your sleep and stress are a mess when the drug's volume knob comes back up, you're fighting your appetite on two fronts. Protect your sleep, especially in the months around stopping.

- Visual: reuse the volume-knob clip from Shot 9 (turning up). Then stock "woman sleeping peacefully morning light", "evening wind down herbal tea".

### Step 5 · Hunger skills

**Shot 41 · 10:35–11:05 · ON CAM, softer**
> Step five: learn your hunger while it's quiet. This is the skill almost nobody practises. While you're on the medicine and the food noise is quiet, notice what it feels like to eat until you're comfortably satisfied, not stuffed. Notice which meals keep you full and which leave you hunting for snacks an hour later. Eat slowly. Stop when you're satisfied.
>
> Write down what you learn. Those are your notes for later, when the volume comes back up.

- Text: **5 · Learn your hunger while it's quiet**
- Edit: a 2–3 second cutaway on "Write down what you learn". Stock "woman writing in journal at kitchen table".

## Shoot plan

### On-camera session (one sitting, about 45–60 minutes)
- **Shots:** 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 11, 12, 17, 18, 20, 22, 23, 26, 27, 28, 30, 33, 35, 37, 38, 41, 44, 45, 46, 47.
- **Two framings:** a **medium** shot (head and shoulders, the default) and a **close-up** for emotional lines (1, 11, 22, 45). If you have one camera, film everything medium and zoom in during editing. Shoot in 4K if your phone allows, so the zoom stays sharp.
- **Outfit and set:** one outfit, solid colour, no fine stripes. Use a warm, uncluttered background; a plant or your book shelf is fine.
- **Teleprompter:** use a free phone teleprompter app (e.g. "PromptSmart Lite" or "BIGVU"). Paste in only the ON CAM lines.
- **Retakes:** do 2 takes of each on-camera shot. Clap once before each take so the editor can find it.

### Voiceover session (about 30 minutes)
- **Shots:** 2, 3, 8, 9, 10, 13, 14, 15, 16, 19, 21, 24, 25, 29, 31, 32, 34, 36, 39, 40, 42.
- **Where to record:** in a small room with soft furnishings (a wardrobe full of clothes works brilliantly). Keep the phone or mic 15–20 cm from your mouth, a little off to the side.
- **Pace:** slightly slower than on camera, about 130–140 words a minute. Smile while you read the warm lines; it shows in the voice.
- **Takes:** read each shot as its own take, with 2 seconds of silence before and after. If you stumble, pause, then restart that sentence. Don't stop the recording.
- **File naming:** `VO-13.m4a`, `VO-14.m4a` and so on, by shot number, so the editor can match them.
