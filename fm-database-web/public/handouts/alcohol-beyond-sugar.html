Zero sugar isn't zero effect
Alcohol, sugar & your metabolism

A common belief is that spirits like whisky, vodka and rum are "safe" because they contain no sugar. The sugar part is true. This guide covers the rest of the picture. Every point comes from a peer-reviewed medical journal, cited at the end.

Grams per serving are worked out from the per-litre figures in the studies below (spirits, wines, sherry and port1; ice wine15; beer 4–5 g carbohydrate per 100 mL14). 1 teaspoon of sugar ≈ 4 g. Mixers are not included: the sugar in cola, tonic, soda or juice adds to the figure above, and the amount is printed on the mixer's label.

For example, whiskey, cognac, and vodka contain no sugars Lieber CS, Alcohol Research & Health, 20031

So if sugar were the only question, spirits would be the safest drinks on the shelf. But alcohol is processed by the liver, the same organ that controls blood sugar and blood fats. That's where its effects come from, whichever drink it arrives in.

Part 2

What alcohol does, with or without sugar

Blood sugar, over time

Regular drinking over the long term pushes blood sugar up in people with diabetes, even when the drink has no sugar.2 When moderate-to-heavy drinkers stopped for one month, their insulin resistance fell by about a quarter (26%). A group that kept drinking saw no such change.3

long-term alcohol use in well-nourished diabetics can result in excessive blood sugar levelsEmanuele NV et al., Alcohol Health & Research World, 19982
Blood sugar, same night

Between meals, the liver keeps blood sugar steady by making new glucose. Alcohol switches this off. In fasting healthy men, it cut the liver's glucose production by 45%.13 This is why drinking on an empty stomach is best avoided.2

diabetics should consume alcohol only with or shortly after mealsEmanuele NV et al., 19982
Blood fats (triglycerides)

After a drink, the liver starts turning alcohol into new fat. In one study, the share of blood fat made this way rose from 2% to 30%.4 In controlled trials, about 30 g of alcohol a day (roughly three small pegs) raised triglycerides.5

The fractional contribution from DNL to VLDL-triacylglycerol palmitate rose after alcohol consumption from 2 +/- 1% to 30 +/- 8%Siler SQ et al., American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 19994
The liver

The first stage of alcohol-related liver damage is fat building up in the liver.1 GGT, a liver enzyme on routine blood tests, rises with regular drinking and comes down when drinking stops.6

CDT and GGT both decreased during 4 weeks of abstinence.Anton RF et al., Alcoholism: Clinical & Experimental Research, 20026
Blood pressure

A review of 36 trials found that cutting down lowers blood pressure in people who drink more than two drinks a day (about 2½ small pegs). People who drank most and halved their intake saw the top number fall by about 5.5 points.7

Reducing alcohol intake lowers blood pressure in a dose-dependent mannerRoerecke M et al., Lancet Public Health, 20177
Acidity & reflux

Across 29 studies, drinkers had about 1.5 times the odds of acid reflux. For people drinking more than 3–5 days a week, the odds were about twice as high.8

The increase in alcohol consumption and frequency showed a stronger association with GERD.Pan J et al., Alcohol and Alcoholism, 20198
Vitamins (folate)

Alcohol lowers folate, a B vitamin, in several ways: the gut absorbs less of it, the liver stores less, and the kidneys pass more out in the urine.9

reduced dietary folate intake, intestinal malabsorption, reduced liver uptake and storageMedici V, Halsted CH, Molecular Nutrition & Food Research, 20139
Diabetes risk in Asians

The "a little alcohol protects against diabetes" finding may not apply to Asian populations, according to the largest review (38 studies, 1.9 million people).16

Reductions in risk among moderate alcohol drinkers may be confined to women and non-Asian populations.Knott C et al., Diabetes Care, 201516
Good to know

Medicines and tobacco

Metformin, one of the most widely used diabetes medicines, has a rare but serious side effect called lactic acidosis. Heavy drinking is one of its known risk factors.10

Risk factors include those that reduce renal elimination of metformin and excessive alcohol consumption.See KC, World Journal of Diabetes, 202410

Alcohol and tobacco. A study of Indian men found that alcohol alone raised the risk of mouth cancer about 2½ times, even in men who had never smoked or chewed tobacco.11 When 17 studies were pooled, tobacco and alcohol together were worse than the two risks multiplied.12

the joint effect between tobacco and alcohol use is greater than multiplicativeHashibe M et al., Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention, 200912

Worked out for a 40% spirit: alcohol weighs 0.79 g per mL and carries 7.1 calories per gram, nearly twice as much as sugar at 4.1 Soda or water keeps the glass sugar-free. Cola, juice or sweet mixers add sugar on top.

Putting it together

What the research points to

  1. Spirits are the lowest-sugar choice, but the alcohol itself still affects blood sugar, blood fats, liver and blood pressure. (1–7)
  2. Always with food, never on an empty stomach. (2, 13)
  3. Keep the mixer sugar-free. Soda or water rather than cola, tonic or juice. (1)
  4. Fewer, smaller pegs, and alcohol-free days. Blood pressure benefits showed up in people who cut back from more than two drinks a day. (7)
  5. A month's break is revealing. In the studies, insulin resistance, blood pressure and liver enzymes improved within four weeks. Timed before a routine blood test, it shows the difference in black and white. (3, 6)

Anyone who has drunk daily for a long time should check with their doctor before stopping suddenly.
